In **multi-class classification** (like digit recognition with 10 classes), the terms "false positive" and "false negative" work differently than in binary classification.

## The Clarification

**True label = 4, Predicted = 2:**
- This is a **misclassification** or **error**.
- But in multi-class terms, it's both:
  - **False Negative for digit 4** (we failed to identify the true 4)
  - **False Positive for digit 2** (we incorrectly predicted 2)

**True label = 4, Predicted = 9:**
- Again, a misclassification.
- **False Negative for digit 4** and **False Positive for digit 9**.

---

## What the Guide Is Really Asking

The guide is nudging you to think: **Are all errors equally bad?**

Consider two scenarios:

| Scenario | True | Predicted | Business Impact |
|----------|------|-----------|-----------------|
| Scenario A | 4 | 2 | Mail goes to ZIP code 2xxx instead of 4xxx → **completely wrong destination** |
| Scenario B | 4 | 9 | Mail goes to ZIP code 9xxx instead of 4xxx → **also completely wrong destination** |

In this case, both are equally catastrophic—the mail goes to the wrong place regardless.

**But** from a *modeling perspective*, you might ask:
- Is confusing 4↔9 more "understandable" (they look similar) than confusing 4↔0 (they look very different)?
- Should you care more about minimizing confusion between similar digits, or is any error equally bad?

---

## Better Terms for Multi-Class Problems

Instead of thinking in binary FP/FN, think in terms of:

1. **Per-class recall:** Of all true 4s, what % did we correctly identify?
   - High recall for 4 = few false negatives (not missing 4s)
   
2. **Per-class precision:** Of all predicted 4s, what % were actually 4?
   - High precision for 4 = few false positives (not wrongly predicting 4)

3. **Confusion pairs:** Which digits do we confuse most? (4↔9, 3↔5, etc.)

---

## For Your Business Context

The real question for **MailSort Inc.** is:

> "**Is any misclassification equally costly, or are some worse than others?**"

- If digit 5 → 3 and digit 5 → 8 both send mail to wrong ZIP codes with similar delivery costs, then all errors are equally bad.
- If there's *no difference*, focus on **overall accuracy** and **per-digit recall** (don't miss any 5s).
- If there *is* a difference (e.g., "confusing 5 with 3 loses $5; confusing 5 with 8 loses $50"), then you'd want to weight errors differently or focus on specific confusions.
