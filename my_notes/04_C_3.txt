Here's the framework:

---

**1. Tree Depth (`max_depth`)**

Definition: How many splits deep each tree can grow.

Why it matters:
- **Deep trees** (no limit): Memorize training data → high overfit, very high train acc, lower val/test acc.
- **Shallow trees**: Underfit, both train and val acc are low.
- **Sweet spot**: Just deep enough to learn patterns without memorizing.

Your choice: `max_depth=10`
- Reasoning: You're limiting growth to 10 levels. This prevents individual trees from memorizing exact training samples while still allowing complex patterns.
- Impact: Reduces overfitting compared to `max_depth=None` (unlimited).

**How to verify:** Compare your train-val gap. If it's small (~1-3%), depth is good. If large (>10%), increase depth.

---

**2. Number of Trees (`n_estimators`)**

Definition: How many decision trees Random Forest trains and averages.

Why it matters:
- **Few trees** (e.g., 10): High variance, predictions are unstable/noisy.
- **Many trees** (e.g., 1000): Smooth, stable predictions. But slower training.
- **Overfitting risk**: Low. More trees = more averaging = less overfitting.

Your choice: `n_estimators=100`
- Reasoning: 100 trees is a standard starting point. Enough to stabilize predictions without excessive computation.
- Impact: Reduces variance through averaging.

**How to verify:** Train with 50, 100, 200. Does validation accuracy improve then plateau? If val acc plateaus at 100, no need for more trees.

---

**3. Min Samples Per Leaf (`min_samples_leaf`)**

Definition: Minimum number of samples required to create a leaf node (a final prediction node in a tree).

Why it matters:
- **Small value** (e.g., 1): Trees grow deep, fit individual samples → overfitting.
- **Large value** (e.g., 50): Trees stop early, don't learn fine patterns → underfitting.
- **Sweet spot**: Prevents trees from splitting on single-sample noise.

Your choice: `min_samples_leaf=5`
- Reasoning: Each leaf must have at least 5 samples before the tree splits further. Prevents overfitting to outliers.
- Impact: Regularizes the model similarly to limiting depth.

**How to verify:** Compare train-val gap. If high, try higher values (10, 20). If both train and val are low, try lower values (1, 2).

Key Learning Point — Why is RF better than LR?

Logistic regression draws one straight line per class through 784 pixel dimensions. It can't learn: "if pixel A AND pixel B are both bright, it's a 3" — that's a nonlinear combination.

Random Forest can: each tree split asks "is pixel X above threshold?" and chains those decisions. This lets it learn combinations of pixels (local patterns), even without explicitly knowing they're neighbors.

What it still can't do: it doesn't know pixels 378 and 379 are near to each other (adjacent) in the image. A CNN would exploit that spatial relationship explicitly.
