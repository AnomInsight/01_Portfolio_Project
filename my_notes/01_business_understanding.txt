Problem framing:
	- Core problem
	- Who are the stakeholders? -> who will use it, pay for it, approve it, maintain it or live with its mistakes?
	- What happens if the model is wrong?
	- What are the constraints?
		○ How fast does it need to run? (real-time, batch, offline)
		○ How much training data do we have or need?
		○ What compute resources are available?
		○ Does the model need to be interpretable to staff? (glass-box)
	- What would success look like?
		○ A single number (e.g. 95% accuracy) Or multiple criteria?
		○ Overall accuracy? Something else?

Part A.1: Business Value and ROI

Benefit description
	○ Estimated benefit (euro)
	○ Estimated effort (euro)
		○ ROI

1. Why is this project worth doing from a business perspective?
   - Does it reduce cost, save time, improve quality, reduce risk, or increase revenue?
   - What process is expected to improve if the solution works?

2. How would the business benefit be described?
   - Time saved per task?
   - Lower error rates?
   - Less manual work?
   - Better customer experience?

3. Is ROI expected to be estimated?
   - You do not need exact numbers at the start.
   - A rough estimate or qualitative statement of value is often enough in early discovery.

4. Who owns the business case?
   - Product owner or department lead?
   - If exact numbers are unavailable, note that as an assumption or something to validate later.

		
Success Criteria and Metrics:
	- Metrics definition
		○ Primary metrics ->
			§ Which metric matters most?
			§ Accuracy? Precision? Recall? F1?
		○ Acceptable threshold -> 
			What value of the primary metric is "good enough"?
			   - 90%? 95%? 99%?
			   - Why that number? Is it realistic?
		○ Secondary metrics -> 
			What else matters?
			   - Inference speed?
			   - Model size?
			   - Interpretability?
		○ Trade-off questions (accuracy 1% up, training time doubled or requires 10x more data to train)

Assumptions and Risks
	- List assumptions about the problem -> Which assumptions might be wrong?
	- Risks
		○ If these assumptions break, what happens?

Non-Goals
	- What are we NOT trying to do? -> (e.g.: NOT trying to achieve 99.5% accuracy (that's beyond learning project scope))

Summary
Write a one-paragraph summary of the problem
